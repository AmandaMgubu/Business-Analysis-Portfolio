# Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)
Moving from the existing state (AS-IS), which is characterized by delays, errors, and manual labor, to a future state (TO-BE) that is quicker, more accurate, and more student-friendly is the goal of managing the student registration process improvement. I will employ KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) to ensure this improvement is quantifiable and long-lasting.

I have chosen five primary KPIs because they accurately represent the issues found (data inaccuracies, inadequate integration, and manual delays). 

### KPIs enable me to:

•	Describe the characteristics of success.

•	Track developments in real time.

•	Determine which areas require additional correction. 

•	Make sure all stakeholders are aware of the improvements.

### Turnaround Time for Registration (TAT)
•	What it measures: The typical amount of time it takes a student to get confirmation of complete registration after applying.

•	Why it matters: Prolonged response times irritate students, raise the likelihood of dropouts, and cause cash flow issues for tuition.

•	Current state (AS-IS): Present condition (AS-IS): Typically, 5–7 working days.

•	Goal (TO-BE): 1-2 days following integration and automation.

•	How to measure: Keep track of the timestamps from the application submission to the confirmation in the Student Management System (SMS).

•	Management: If TAT exceeds the goal, I will look at bottlenecks (financing checks, verification delays, or IT downtime).

### Rate of Data Accuracy
•	What it measures: It calculates the proportion of registrations and applications processed without errors, missing fields, or rework.

•	Why it matters: Mistakes result in reprocessing, additional staff time, and unhappy students.

•	Current state (AS-IS): Accuracy is between 70 and 75 percent; admittance, financing, and academics frequently don't match.

•	Goal (Future state): ≥95% accuracy.

•	How to measure: Measurement methods include the quantity of denied or corrected applications and error reports from SMS.

•	Management: If accuracy declines, I will investigate whether the issue stems from manual collection, system integration problems, or unclear student requirements.

### Success Rate for System Integration
•	What it measures: The percentage of data successfully transferred between academic, financial, and admissions platforms without human interaction.

•	Why it matters: Process improvement relies heavily on integration; mistakes here lead to inconsistencies, delays, and redundancy.

•	Current state (AS-IS): Less than 50% automated; manual transfers and compartmentalized systems.

Goal (Future state): 100% smooth integration following enhancements.

•	How to measure: Monitor error alarms and system sync logs between the academic, financial, and SMS systems.

•	Management: IT and vendors will immediately be notified to fix interface or API problems if integration faults arise.

### Score for Student Satisfaction
•	What it measures: How easy, straightforward, quick, and communicative students think the registration procedure is.

•	Why it matters: The final consumers are students. Enrollment and retention may suffer from a bad registration experience.

•	Current state (AS-IS): Conflicting opinions, many grievances regarding delays, and insufficient updates.

•	Goal (Future state): at least 85% satisfaction level.

•	How to measure: Net Promoter Score (NPS) tracking and email/portal post-registration surveys are two measurement methods.

•	Management: I will address pain points, such as confusing instructions, poor communication, or system outages, if satisfaction declines.

### Rate of Registration Completion

•	What it measures: The proportion of students who begin the registration process and successfully finish it.

•	Why it matters: A low completion rate must suggest administrative, financial, or technical obstacles that lead students to drop out of the course.

•	Current state (AS-IS): 60% completion.

•	Goal (Future state): 90% or more.

•	How to measure: CRM/SMS analytics (applications received vs. successful registrations) is one way to measure.

•	Management: I will investigate why students drop out (payment, document submission, or system issues) if completion rates are poor.


I will oversee the procedure using a continuous improvement cycle aligned with SREDIM (Select, Record, Examine, Develop, Implement, Maintain). This cycle is connected to every KPI:
•	Choose - Determine which aspects of registration require attention, such as data inaccuracies or a sluggish turnaround. KPIs like Data Accuracy and TAT highlight the worst-performing regions.
•	Record: To comprehend the AS-IS performance, record - capture baseline data for each KPI (example, current TAT = 7 days).
•	Examine - Determine the core causes of poor KPI performance using the Fishbone, Pareto, and Five Whys frameworks.
•	Create - Design solutions (e.g., integration, automation, training). I will set new KPI goals to account for anticipated advancements.
•	Implement enhancements (self-service tracking, automation, and integration). Throughout the rollout, monitor KPIs.
•	Maintain - Maintain an eye on KPIs to maintain gains. I will examine the underlying reasons and take corrective action if KPI targets are unmet.

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs) will serve as the primary monitoring and control tool to oversee the efficient enhancement of the student registration procedure. The current issues of delays, mistakes, and inadequate system integration are immediately addressed by the chosen KPIs: registration turnaround time, data accuracy rate, system integration success rate, student happiness score, and registration completion rate. From cutting the registration period from seven days to two, to enhancing data accuracy to over 95%, guaranteeing smooth system integration, and raising student satisfaction and completion rates, each KPI offers a quantifiable means of monitoring success. The SREDIM framework will manage these KPIs, including identifying problem areas, gathering baseline data, investigating underlying causes, creating solutions, implementing changes, and sustaining gains through ongoing monitoring. The improvement process will be open, accountable, and consistent with the university's value offer of "quick, reliable access to learning" by connecting each KPI to stakeholder responsibilities and system performance. In this sense, KPIs function as a strategic tool for ongoing process improvement in addition to being performance metrics.
