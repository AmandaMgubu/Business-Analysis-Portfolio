# RESULTS, RECOMMENDATIONS, CONCLUSION

## Results
Analytical methods (Fishbone, Pareto, SWOT, SIPOC, W5/xBML, and SREDIM) revealed that manual processing, inadequate system integration, and inconsistent data across departments significantly limited the current registration process. According to the Pareto analysis, data inaccuracies, sluggish handovers between finance and admissions, and human document verification were the only underlying causes of 80% of registration delays. Contributing elements like compartmentalized IT systems, unclear personnel duties, and misaligned policies were also revealed by the Fishbone diagram. Although the university has a compelling value proposition and a high demand for blended learning, its operational flaws and inefficiencies make it less competitive, according to the SWOT analysis. The misalignment of suppliers, inputs, and outputs was validated by the SIPOC model, especially when data is transferred between admissions and finance. These findings clearly show the current situation: a disjointed, manual procedure that irritates students and slows down income flow.

## Recommendations
•	System integration: To cut down on data silos and manual transfers, implement a centralized Student Management System (SMS) that relates to academic, financial, and CRM platforms.
•	Automated verification: Reduce the need for manual inspections and increase student transparency by implementing automated document verification and status tracking.
•	Process simplification: Process simplification involves redesigning workflows to eliminate unnecessary stages and using BPMN modeling to define roles.
•	Building capacity: Educate finance and admissions personnel on new systems while maintaining role clarity and preventing single points of failure.
•	KPI monitoring: To keep the momentum of improvement going, continuously monitor student happiness, data accuracy, and registration turnaround time.
•	Change management and communication: Implement proactive communication channels (email updates and SMS) and explicit change management methods to lessen student uncertainty.
 

## Conclusion
A key value chain component is the registration process, which is essential to the student experience and long-term financial viability. Both are at risk due to current inefficiencies. The institution can make its registration process a quick, dependable, and student-focused service by implementing the suggested enhancements, system integration, automation, and KPI-driven monitoring. This will ensure immediate operational efficiency and long-term institutional competitiveness by lowering dropout rates, improving satisfaction, and raising enrollment conversion rates.
