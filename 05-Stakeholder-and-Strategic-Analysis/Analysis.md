# Stakeholder & Strategic Analysis

## 1. Purpose

Stakeholder and strategic analysis was used to understand the people and organisational factors that influence the student registration process.

The analysis considers:

- Stakeholder roles and interests
- Stakeholder involvement in the process
- Organisational strengths and weaknesses
- External opportunities and threats
- How the registration process contributes to value creation

## 2. Stakeholder Analysis

The registration process involves several stakeholders who contribute to or are affected by the process.

| Stakeholder | Role / Interest | Key Need |
|---|---|---|
| Students | Apply for registration and receive the final outcome | Simple process, timely communication and visibility |
| Admissions | Receive applications and verify documentation | Complete and accurate information |
| Finance | Manage fee information and payment confirmation | Accurate payment information and timely confirmation |
| Academic Departments | Review and approve registration | Complete information for decision-making |
| Student Administration | Coordinate registration activities and maintain records | Accurate records and process visibility |
| IT Department | Support systems and technical functionality | Reliable systems and clear technical requirements |
| University Management | Monitor operational performance and outcomes | Process efficiency and measurable performance |

## 3. Stakeholder Considerations

The analysis shows that the registration process is dependent on coordination between multiple stakeholders.

A delay or information problem at one stage can affect activities performed by other stakeholders.

Improving the process therefore requires consideration of:

- Clear responsibilities
- Accurate information
- Effective communication
- Timely hand-offs
- Process visibility
- Stakeholder needs

## 4. SWOT Analysis

A SWOT Analysis was used to consider internal and external factors that may influence the registration process.

### Strengths

- Existing registration process and organisational structure
- Involvement of dedicated administrative departments
- Existing digital platforms and technology resources
- Established registration activities and responsibilities

### Weaknesses

- Manual processing activities
- Multiple departmental hand-offs
- Limited process visibility
- Potential information inconsistencies
- Communication delays

### Opportunities

- Increased process automation
- Improved system integration
- Electronic document submission
- Automated notifications
- Process performance monitoring

### Threats

- Increased registration volumes
- System availability issues
- Data quality problems
- Delays caused by departmental dependencies
- Resistance to changes in existing processes

## 5. Business Model Canvas

The Business Model Canvas was used to understand the broader value created by the university's registration process and the relationships between its key business elements.

The analysis considers areas such as:

- Key stakeholders and partners
- Key activities and resources
- Value proposition
- Student relationships and channels
- Cost and value considerations

For this project, the relevant value proposition is:

> **Quick and reliable access to learning.**

The registration process contributes to this value proposition by providing students with access to programmes and supporting services.

## 6. Business Analysis Insight

The stakeholder and strategic analysis demonstrates that process improvement should consider more than operational efficiency.

A successful improvement should also consider:

- Who is affected by the process
- What each stakeholder requires
- How the process supports organisational value
- Internal constraints and opportunities
- External factors that may affect process performance

These findings support the development of requirements and future-state improvements.
